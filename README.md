# Maternal Mortality Analysis (U.S.)

## 📌 Project Overview
This project analyzes maternal mortality trends in the United States, with a focus on understanding how rates changed before, during, and after the COVID-19 pandemic. The analysis also explores demographic, socioeconomic, and healthcare access factors contributing to maternal mortality disparities.

## 🎯 Research Questions
- How did maternal mortality rates change during and following the COVID-19 pandemic compared to pre-pandemic years?
- What demographic and socioeconomic factors are associated with higher maternal mortality rates in the U.S.?

## 🗂 Data Sources
- CDC Provisional Maternal Death Counts and Rates (2019–2023)
- Pregnancy Risk Assessment Monitoring System (PRAMS) (2018–2022)
- CDC Pregnancy Mortality Surveillance System (2017–2020)
- WHO Maternal Mortality References

## 🛠 Tools & Technologies
- Excel (data extraction, merging, pivot tables)
- Python (data cleaning, EDA, correlation analysis)
- Tableau (interactive data visualizations)
- Public health datasets (CDC, WHO)

## 🔄 Methodology
- Identified and evaluated multiple public health datasets
- Cleaned and standardized multi-year data across sources
- Merged datasets and validated data quality
- Conducted exploratory data analysis (EDA)
- Performed correlation analysis between mortality ratios, live births, and death counts
- Built visualizations to highlight trends and disparities

## 📊 Key Analyses & Visualizations
- Maternal mortality trends over time (pre- vs post-COVID)
- Maternal mortality rate by age group
- Maternal mortality rate by race/ethnicity
- Urban vs rural maternal mortality comparisons
- Correlation matrix showing relationships between key variables

## 📈 Key Findings
- Maternal mortality rates increased during and after the COVID-19 pandemic.
- Strong positive correlation (~0.98) between maternal mortality ratio and maternal death counts.
- Black and American Indian/Alaska Native mothers experience disproportionately higher mortality rates.
- Women aged 40 and older face significantly higher maternal mortality risk.
- Rural areas show higher mortality rates due to limited healthcare access and infrastructure.

## ⚠️ Challenges
- Data availability and quality inconsistencies across sources
- Complexity in integrating PRAMS, CDC VSRR, and PMSS datasets
- Limited real-time post-pandemic data
- Interpreting socioeconomic and demographic interactions

## ✅ Key Takeaways
- Many maternal deaths are preventable with improved prenatal and postpartum care.
- Data-driven insights can inform targeted healthcare policies.
- Addressing healthcare access and equity is critical to reducing maternal mortality disparities.
